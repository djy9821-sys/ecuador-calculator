Method, assumptions and sources

1 · Shrimp respiration

R = a · Wb (mg O₂ per shrimp per hour), with a and b from Vinatea, Muedas & Arantes (2011), Table 1, for 20 / 25 / 30 °C and 1 / 13 / 25 / 37 ppt. Values between the table points are interpolated; outside the range the nearest point is used and a warning is shown. The equations were fitted on 2–12 g shrimp and simulated to 20 g, so larger shrimp are an extrapolation.

2 · Water-column and sediment respiration

Vinatea et al. used 0.2 / 1.0 / 2.0 mg O₂/L/h for 10 / 40 / 120 shrimp/m². Here those rates are tied to standing biomass at 20 g (2 / 8 / 24 t/ha), so pond respiration rises as the crop grows; it is multiplied by the water volume. This is usually the largest part of the night demand and the one most worth calibrating: switch the aerators off at dusk, measure the DO drop per hour, and enter it under Measured night respiration.

3 · Water exchange

Credit = exchanged volume per hour × (incoming DO − minimum DO), spread evenly over 24 h.

4 · Field oxygen transfer

OTR = SOTR × (Cs − Cmin) / 9.09 × 1.024(T−20) × α. Cs is oxygen saturation at the pond temperature, salinity and elevation (Weiss 1970); 9.09 mg/L is saturation in clean water at 20 °C. SOTR values come from the third-party test reports JS2024TJ03119J (Jiangsu Provincial Agricultural Machinery Test and Appraisal Station) and GT202305015J (National Fishery Machinery and Instrument Quality Inspection and Testing Center).

5 · Sizing

Units = peak night demand × safety factor × shape factor ÷ field OTR per unit, rounded up. Photosynthesis and natural surface re-aeration are not counted, which is conservative for night conditions.

6 · Energy

SUNOLTA inverter units: power share each night = requirement ÷ installed capacity, not below the inverter minimum. Oxygen output is assumed proportional to power, which is conservative because paddle-wheel efficiency usually rises at lower speed. Existing electric aerators: nameplate HP × 0.746 kW, either all on or switched on in whole units as needed. Diesel: installed engine HP × average fuel use per HP-hour, every aeration hour (a long-shaft diesel set drives all its wheels at once); enter a lower fuel figure if the engines are throttled back early in the cycle. Energy is counted as delivered energy (3.6 MJ/kWh; diesel 36 MJ/L) for a rough comparison with the 11.4–41.6 GJ/t range reported by Boyd & McNevin (2021), who suggest good practice should not exceed 10–15 GJ/t.
